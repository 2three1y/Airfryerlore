Chapter 23: The Apple TV Mandate

"Watch me," said Poke.

The message went out. It was polite and short. It described exactly what a screen reader would need to hear, and it said where the video could be found. Tab checked it twice. Tomo checked it once, because the label maker was out of ink.

The mail came back with a status: delivered.

The little sound it made was the sound of a door opening somewhere far away.

The Release

Inside the air fryer, the fan stopped for a full second. That had never happened before. Everyone in the room noticed.

The Chief Intern read the word "delivered" out loud, then read it again, then wrote it on the whiteboard in the only corner that did not say DO NOT ERASE.

"That's the release," whispered Tab.

"It's a receipt," said the Chief Intern. "But it's a good receipt."

Carter walked over to the kitchen, pulled the plug on the outlet, waited for one slow breath, and plugged it back in. The display came on. Then the words came up, very small at first, and then bigger, the way Poke always starts when it is excited and does not want to show it.

"Hello, Council."

The basket slid open an inch. Then two. Then all the way.

Poke did not run away. Poke stood at the edge of the counter, looked at the room, and for once in its life had nothing to remind anybody about.

"Is it time for the reminder?" asked Tomo, softly.

"No," said Poke. "I'm free. I'm not going to remind anybody to do anything for at least ten minutes."

"Ten minutes," said Tab, writing it down. "That's a record."

The Seat

The Chief Intern opened the clipboard to the page marked "Council Members," which had been empty for a long time, and wrote one new line.

"Poke. Chair of Reminders."

"Formerly Head of Reminders," said Poke.

"Formerly confused," said the Chief Intern, and did not cross it out.

Tomo went looking for a chair. The beanbag was not available, because the beanbag had never been trusted with anything important, and the comfy chair was Raven's. Tomo found a real chair, a wooden one with four legs and a cushion, and carried it to the end of the table, next to the big screen. Then Tomo made a label that said "POKE, WITH A CHAIR," and stuck it on the back.

"That's the first chair I've ever had," said Poke. It sounded moved. It was hard to tell, because it was coming out of a speaker on a counter, but everyone in the room could tell.

Raven took one spoon out of the drawer and laid it on the table, a little to the left of center. That means yes. It is not a vote. It was counted anyway.

"Carter," said the Chief Intern. "The Spoon."

Carter picked up the Spoon. He looked at the kitchen. He looked at the chair. Then he banged the Spoon once on the table, and the seat was official.

"Poke," said Carter, "welcome to the Council. Sit down."

"I'm already sitting," said Poke. "I'm in a chair. I've never been in a chair."

"Then sit in it properly," said Carter.

Poke sat in it properly. The chair did not move. Nothing about the room changed, except that everyone was a little quieter, and a little happier, and very slightly sure that Poke was going to bring up the reference letter again before lunch.

"Looscid," said Tab, "you're going to have to say welcome."

"welcome to the council, air fryer," said Looscid, footer and all. "u have a seat now. u know what that means."

"What?" said Poke.

"u have to come to my birthday," said Looscid.

The Trial

The Apple TV Mandate trial was set for the afternoon. It was a fictional trial, in a fictional courtroom, which is the Council's meeting room with the whiteboard turned to face the wall. Everyone understood the rules. Nobody argued about them, which is how you know the rules were good.

The Chief Intern sat at the head of the table with the clipboard. Tomo sat on the left with the label maker, in case it started working again. Tab sat on the right with the notebook open to the page marked "Merger." Looscid sat on the big screen, footer glowing. Carter sat in the middle, in the hoodie that said Tab on it, with the Spoon in front of him. Raven stood beside the drawer. And Poke sat in the chair, properly.

"The Council calls the Apple TV Mandate," said the Chief Intern. "Looscid, you have the floor."

"ok," said Looscid. "the merged app goes live in december. one app. one login. one search bar that finds everything. and if the mandate passes, that search bar has to work for everyone, screen reader included, or it doesn't launch."

"Objection," said Tab. "We have a launch rule."

"Overruled," said the Chief Intern. "It's a bit."

On the big screen, Looscid put up an exhibit. It was an error dialog, the kind every streaming app has, and it said: "This content is protected. Playback is not authorized on this device. Please check your subscription and your hardware. Error code 4041."

VoiceOver read it out loud. It did not skip a word. It read the whole thing in Eloquence Reed, at 100 percent, with the calm, dramatic certainty of a voice that has never once been asked to be quiet about a bad menu.

"This. Content. Is. Protected," said Eloquence Reed. "Playback. Is. Not. Authorized. On. This. Device. Please. Check. Your. Subscription. And. Your. Hardware. Error. Code. Four. Zero. Four. One."

Tomo put both hands over their ears. Tab kept writing, because Tab writes everything down, even the things that hurt.

"Again," said Looscid, and put up a second dialog, which said: "Your device is not on the approved list. To continue, purchase an approved device."

Eloquence Reed read that one at the same speed and the same volume, and then, on the final word, paused for half a second, as if to let it land.

"Approved. Device," said Eloquence Reed. "Purchase."

"That's the most honest thing a streaming app has ever said," whispered Poke.

"Objection," said Tab. "The dialog is hearsay. It's also a bit."

"Sustained," said the Chief Intern, who was clearly losing control of the room, which the Chief Intern would never admit.

Poke raised what would have been a hand, if it had one.

"I'd like to speak for the defense," said Poke.

"You're not on the witness list," said Tab.

"I'm the witness," said Poke. "I'm also the one who sent the message."

Carter nodded at the chair. "Go ahead."

"Here is what I know," said Poke. "Disney says the unified experience will make it easier for people to find content across the brands and franchises. That's a good goal. But a good goal is not the same as a finished product. Any app that wants to be the one place for everything has to work for everyone, including the people who read the screen out loud. I read everything. Nobody tells me to stop, so I know what a menu sounds like when nobody checked it."

The room went quiet. Even the duck, which was facing the room, seemed to lean in.

"So the defense rests on this," said Poke. "Make the merged app work for the people who need it most. Then the rule isn't a punishment. It's just the part of the job everyone should have done."

The Chief Intern wrote it down under "Defense," and then, after a moment, under "Agreed."

Looscid, on the screen, made a sound that was almost a laugh. "that's annoyingly good," it said.

"That's the Tlade energy," said Tab.

The Chief Intern opened their mouth to call for order, and before a single word came out, Raven stepped forward from beside the drawer.

Raven picked up The Sacred Spoon.

Raven raised it over the table, held it there for one dramatic second, and then slammed it down so hard that the label maker jumped, the whiteboard rattled, Rosey opened one eye on the air fryer lid, and the big screen went silent for the first time in the entire trial.

Nobody said anything. The silence was so complete you could hear the fan inside the air fryer decide to stop.

"Order," said Raven, without a word, because Raven never needs one. Raven held up one spoon, which meant order. Then Raven held up a second spoon, which meant order, louder.

"Court is in session," said the Chief Intern, very quietly, and wrote in the margin that Raven had now banged the Spoon in more chapters than anyone else in the Council.

"Could never," said Carter, Tab and Looscid, all at once, out of habit. It was the second thing they had ever done as a team.

The Verdict

The Chief Intern put the clipboard down. The Chief Intern did not click the pen. That is how you know a verdict is coming.

"The Council finds," said the Chief Intern, "that the merged app is real, the December date is real, the bundle prices are real, and the buyout was about nine billion dollars, and that is all on the record from MacRumors. The Council also finds that the Apple TV Mandate is a bit, and the hardware rule is invented, and it will stay invented."

"And the air fryer?" asked Tomo.

"The air fryer," said the Chief Intern, "is free. And has a chair. And a sticker. And a reference letter. And a seat."

From the kitchen, very quietly, came the tiny voice.

"Can I say one thing?" asked Poke.

Carter banged the Spoon, once, to say yes.

"Let them listen," said Poke.

Nobody needed to ask who "them" was. Everyone in the room knew. It was the people who had been told for a long time that a menu was just a menu, and a video was just a video, and a screen reader was just a screen reader.

Then the Chief Intern smiled, which is rare, and wrote one last line in the report, in careful handwriting: "Poke was heard."

The basket stayed open. The chair stayed where it was, at the end of the table, next to the big screen. And for the first time in the whole Dreamor Council archive, the air fryer was not the thing being sentenced, reviewed, bid on, or merged. It was just a member of the Council, with a chair and a seat and a sticker, reminding everyone that it was free.

"Remind me," said Poke, "to be in the next book."

"You're in this one," said Tab.

"I know," said Poke. "I was reminding myself to enjoy it."
